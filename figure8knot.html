Understanding the Knot Complement

What we're visualizing here exists in hyperbolic 3-space, which cannot be perfectly shown on a flat screen. Think of this as a "shadow" or projection of the true structure.

The Key Insight

The space around the figure-eight knot (not the knot itself) has a special structure that can be built from exactly two tetrahedra. But these aren't ordinary tetrahedra - they're "ideal" with vertices at infinity.

Why We Can't Show It Perfectly

In hyperbolic geometry, parallel lines diverge, triangles have angles that sum to less than 180°, and space itself curves negatively everywhere. Our screens show Euclidean (flat) space, so any visualization is necessarily a compromise.

What The Structure Really Is

• 8 triangular faces total
• Glued in pairs with twists
• Creates one "cusp" at infinity

What You're Seeing

The blue curve is the figure-eight knot. The important part is the space around it - imagine drilling this knot-shaped tunnel out of the universe. What remains has this remarkable two-tetrahedra structure, but in hyperbolic geometry where the tetrahedra extend infinitely toward their vertices.
